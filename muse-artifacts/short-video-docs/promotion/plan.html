Short-Video Promotion & Advertising — Plan

Owner: Balram Bandhu · Built & operated by: Muse Bandhu Date: 2026-09-23 · Updated: 2026-09-24 — locked decisions incorporated Status: Draft for review (awaits pilot winners) Technical spec: techspec.md Tasks: tasks-balram.md (decisions & approvals — yours) · tasks-muse.md (build work — Muse's) Parent pipeline: ../create-and-publish/plan.md (builds the content) Locked decisions: ../reference/decisions-log.md · Monetization research: ../reference/monetization-research.md

This document covers the promotion pipeline only — one of five independent pipelines, each with its own codebase, its own docs, and its own observability: create-and-publish, promotion (this), housekeeping, performance-monitoring, and earnings-improvement. They share a learning knowledge base (see §9).

1. Objective

Min cost, max impact. Every dollar of paid spend must beat its organic benchmark or it gets cut. Paid never substitutes for organic — it amplifies proven winners only.

2. Principles

  1. Fund winners only. No cold creative ever gets budget. A video earns paid support by performing organically first (top-decile velocity / retention in its first 48h).
  2. Test → validate → scale → harvest. Budgets move up a fixed ladder on measured efficiency, never on hunches.
  3. Kill fast. Losers are paused by rule, not debated.
  4. One metric per campaign type: cost per view (reach campaigns), cost per follower/subscriber (growth campaigns).
  5. Bounded by default. Every campaign has a default duration and budget; anything bigger is an explicit, approved override.
  6. Reports separate organic results, paid results, and measured relationships — never blended into one number.

3. Organic engine (always-on, $0)

The pipeline enforces these by template; promotion owns the tuning:

  • Reels/Shorts craft: hook <3s, burned-in captions, 9:16 full-bleed, original audio, niche + broad hashtags.
  • Stories flywheel: every Reel reshared to Stories.
  • Native cross-posting: each video ships as a separate native upload to IG and the FB Page (never cross-links — platforms demote them).
  • First-hour engagement (defined workflow): at publish +15/+30/+45 min, Muse pings you with new comments; you reply within the hour (or approve Muse-drafted replies where the API allows). A question-comment is seeded at publish to start discussion. Full runbook: pipeline tech task T2.5.
  • Trending-audio swap (your 2-minute job): the pipeline ships with original audio — commercial trending sounds can't be attached via API. For proven winners, you open the video natively in TikTok/IG, swap in the trending sound, and repost. Muse flags which winners deserve it and suggests the sound. Manual, but only ever for winners.
  • YouTube-native: keyword-first titles, #Shorts, pinned question comment, theme playlists, title A/B variants logged for review.
  • Monthly collab: one collab Reel with a same-niche creator (Muse shortlists, you approve outreach).
  • Winner recycling: monthly cutdowns, "best of" compilations, and 30-day reposts of proven videos with fresh hooks — winners keep working (pipeline builds it; promotion picks the winners from R2).
  • Remix/response videos: weekly shortlist of breakout videos from larger in-niche creators; approved targets become original response takes with credit — borrowed audience, your spin.
  • TikTok distribution: every video ships natively to TikTok alongside YT/IG/FB — strong organic reach per post in short-form (benchmarked at build; pipeline tech spec §1.4).

Optional levers (not committed — revisit when the core is humming): episodic series (Part 1/2/3), a hard retention-gate rule (kill formats under ~30% 3-second retention), external seeding (niche subreddits, X clips), community features at thresholds (YT Community tab, IG broadcast channel).

Exact automation support per tactic is verified at build (tech spec §6) — anything the APIs can't do becomes a manual step, never a silent skip.

4. Paid engine — Meta (Instagram + Facebook)

Two campaign types: reach (video views on a winning Reel) and growth (follows). One campaign per hero video; Reels + Stories placements; 9:16 creative as-is.

Scale up / scale down ladder (thresholds finalized in P0.1):

Stage Budget Duration Advance if
Probe $5/day 3 days Cost per view/follower under threshold
Validate $15/day 7 days Efficiency holds within 20% of probe
Scale $30–50/day rolling Cost stays under threshold

Automatic scale-down / kill triggers:

  • Cost ≥ 1.5× threshold for 48h → budget halved, you're notified.
  • Cost ≥ 2× threshold for 48h → paused, you're notified.
  • Two consecutive scale-downs → killed, learnings logged.
  • Creative fatigue (frequency >3.5 with falling engagement) → retired, a variant cut from the winner.

Scale-downs and pauses inside these rules are automatic with notification; scale-ups past 2× always need your approval.

5. Paid engine — Google (YouTube)

  • Campaign: Video Views objective on an already-published Short, Shorts placement, vertical <60s creative, sound-on.
  • Timing: launch soon after publish to catch the early window. (Paid views don't count toward monetization thresholds; the organic views they trigger do.)
  • Targeting: broad affinity/in-market around the niche first, then competitor-keyword segments; mobile only; frequency caps.
  • Warm layer: separate retargeting campaign for viewers of previous videos — the cheapest conversions.
  • Same ladder and auto-brakes as Meta (§4).

6. Min-cost, max-impact tactics

  1. Winners-only funding — the single biggest saver; most videos never see a dollar.
  2. Dayparting: spend concentrated in the channel's proven hours, not 24/7.
  3. Geo layering: 1–2 best geos first; expand only on efficiency.
  4. Warm before cold: retarget engagers before prospecting strangers.
  5. Lookalikes built from video engagers, not broad interest buckets.
  6. Creative recycling: one hero → cheap cutdowns for probe tests.
  7. Free cross-promo: each channel's community tab and Stories push the others — $0 reach.
  8. Frequency discipline: caps stop you paying to annoy people.

7. Campaign defaults & per-campaign overrides

  • Defaults: 7-day duration, $10/day, thresholds per §4, monthly cap set in P0.1.
  • Overrides: any campaign may override duration, budget, or geo — every override is logged with who changed what, when.
  • Single-prompt controls: "pause all ads", "double the fitness channel's budget", "extend finance-ep12 by 7 days", "kill anything over $0.05 CPV", "how much did we spend this month?".

8. Reporting — this pipeline's own reports + shared rollups

This pipeline defines its own operational reports (built in tech task P1.4): a weekly ad response report (per campaign: spend, impressions, views, follows/subs, cost per view/follower, frequency, dates vs defaults), a spend-vs-cap report (pacing against the monthly cap, projected month-end), and a kill-trigger log (every auto scale-down, pause, and kill with the rule that fired and the learnings logged). Affiliate reports (clicks, conversions, revenue per link/video) are added once affiliate programs are live.

These feed the shared R1/R2/R3 cross-pipeline rollups owned by the performance-monitoring pipeline's reporting framework — one framework, many contributors:

  • R1 — Organic impact & popularity. Per-video velocity, retention, engagement rate, follower/subscriber attribution, format leaderboard, breakout flags. Weekly. (Organic data comes from the create-and-publish pipeline's metrics feed.)
  • R2 — Ad response. Per campaign: spend, impressions, views, follows/subs, cost per view/follower, frequency, dates vs defaults. Weekly. (This pipeline's data.)
  • R3 — Organic × paid relation. Incremental lift vs a matched organic baseline; growth in the 7 days after spend stopped; blended cost per 1k views; a keep/cut verdict per boost. Weekly. (Joint create-and-publish + promotion data.)

Delivery: chat digest in the project side chat; full tables under reports/. Videos cited by ref (<channel>.yyyy.MM.dd.N) everywhere. Organic results, paid results, and measured relationships are always reported separately — never blended.

9. Approvals, guardrails & self-governance

  • Muse proposes every campaign (creative, budget, duration, targeting); you approve each launch and any scale-up past 2×.
  • Scale-downs/pauses inside the rules are automatic with notification.
  • Anomaly brake: spend pacing >150% of expected → auto-pause + instant alert.
  • Hard monthly cap — campaigns cannot exceed it, individually or combined.
  • Execution is semi-manual through the Ads Manager / Google Ads web UIs (Muse drives, you can watch) — proposals automatic, launches approved.
  • Sponsor/affiliate manual gates: affiliate program applications, sponsor outreach, negotiation, and closing are yours; identity/tax details are never handled by Muse. Muse prepares shortlists, drafts, tracking, and revenue attribution.

This pipeline's own observability

Independent of the other four pipelines; shared patterns are written back to the learning knowledge base.

  • Own tracking: every campaign event, rule evaluation, brake trigger, spend anomaly, and reporting failure is logged against this pipeline.
  • Failure-pattern history: recurring patterns captured and used — e.g. which creative types fatigue fastest, which geos burn budget, what UI changes broke the browser automation last time.
  • Self-healing (bounded): predefined mechanisms with automated execution plans — auto scale-down/pause/kill per the ladder rules, retry budgets on report pulls, quarantine of suspect metrics. Repeated healing failures escalate instead of looping.
  • Anomaly detection: watches for unfavorable conditions and exception scenarios — spend pacing anomalies, a campaign's efficiency collapsing, API/UI breakage in the browser automation, reporting gaps.
  • Severity-tiered alerts: P1 = anomaly brake fired (spend >150% of expected — auto-paused, you alerted instantly); P2 = kill triggers and repeated brake events needing your call; P3 = informational (creative fatigue retirements, ladder advancements). Tiers are set by importance, occurrence rate, urgency, and the need for human review, approval, or intervention — thresholds locked with you in P0.1.

10. Costs

Tooling $0. Spend = your caps. Probe $5/day, validate $15/day, scale $30–50/day. Suggested pilot month: $150–300 total across Meta + Google.

11. Monetization targets — views & subscribers → CAD income

How short-form actually pays, ranked by realism. (Full research — creator revenue mix, sponsor-rate benchmarks, affiliate economics, and caveats — lives in ../reference/monetization-research.md.)

  1. Brand sponsorships — the real engine. Micro-influencer rates run roughly $25–$50 CAD per 1,000 followers per sponsored post (finance/tech niches pay the top end). One sponsored post a month at 50k followers ≈ $1,250–$2,500.
  2. YouTube Shorts ad revenue — real but thin. Shorts pay from a pooled fund, roughly $0.05–$0.15 USD per 1,000 views (≈ $0.07–$0.21 CAD; working figure $0.14 CAD). Requires 1,000 subs + 10M Shorts views in 90 days before a single dollar flows.
  3. Affiliate — niche-dependent; finance/tech links can out-earn ad revenue per view by 10× or more.
  4. Platform bonuses/gifts — small and shrinking; treat as a bonus, never the plan.

What it takes (working model)

Monthly income target Via Shorts ads alone Via audience (the realistic path)
CAD 100 ~0.7M Shorts views/mo Monetization unlocked: 1k subs + 10M views/90d
CAD 500 ~3.6M views/mo ~15–25k followers + 1 sponsor/mo
CAD 1,500 ~11M views/mo ~50k followers + 2 sponsors/mo
CAD 5,000 ~36M views/mo ~100–150k followers + 2–3 sponsors + affiliate
CAD 10,000 ~71M views/mo 250k+ followers + sponsors + affiliate + own funnel

Takeaway: Shorts ad revenue alone is a weak engine — the audience column is how this actually pays. Paid promotion doesn't buy income directly; it buys audience faster, and the audience buys the income.

Milestones with associated promo cost

Income below is gross revenue — net is gross minus the promo spend shown alongside it. (Media generation isn't counted here: it draws on your existing Muse plan, no incremental cash outlay.)

Milestone Gross income/mo Audience target Promo spend/mo Net income/mo
M1 — Monetized CAD ~100 1k subs, 10M Shorts views/90d $150–300 ≈ −$125 (investment phase)
M2 — First sponsors CAD ~500 15–25k followers $300–500 ≈ +$100
M3 — Steady CAD ~1,500 ~50k followers $500–900 ≈ +$800
M4 — Full-time-ish CAD ~5,000 100–150k followers $900–1,500 ≈ +$3,800
M5 — Scale CAD ~10,000 250k+ followers $1,500–3,000 ≈ +$7,750

Net uses mid-range promo spend. M1 being net-negative is expected and normal — you're buying the audience asset (subscribers/followers), which is what all later milestones monetize. Break-even lands around M2; from M3 on, promo is roughly 30–60% of gross and falling as organic compounds.

Why the promo math works: at a $0.02 cost per view, $300/mo buys only ~15k paid views — nowhere near the millions in the table. It works because paid amplifies proven organic winners (the entire strategy), and followers compound: each winner's audience makes the next video cheaper to push. Organic does the heavy lifting; paid shortens the road. How much organic lift each paid dollar actually produces is measured, not assumed — that's what the R3 report exists to verify. When paid doesn't lift organic, the ladder kills the spend within days.

Can ad spend buy these views? (honest math)

Yes — both platforms let you pay directly for views. Meta: boost a Reel or run a Video Views / Engagement campaign (Reels + Stories placements). Google: a Video Views campaign on your published Short, served in the Shorts feed. That's exactly what the paid engine (§4–§5) does.

But can the budgets above buy the view counts in the table? No — not even close:

Milestone Views needed/mo What the promo budget buys (@ $0.02/view) Share of target
M1 0.7M ~11k views ~2%
M2 3.6M ~20k views ~0.6%
M3 11M ~35k views ~0.3%
M4 36M ~60k views ~0.2%
M5 71M ~112k views ~0.16%

~98%+ of the views must come from organic distribution. Paid is a catalyst, not the fuel: a $5–15/day push behind a proven winner buys initial velocity and follower conversions; if the content earns it, organic distribution follows — the R3 report measures exactly how much lift each paid dollar produced, so we keep what works and cut what doesn't. That's the winners-only rule doing its job.

Same story for followers: at ~$1 per follower, $400/mo buys ~400 followers — M2's 15–25k followers must overwhelmingly come from organic breakouts, with paid accelerating the winners. The R3 report exists precisely to verify this: it measures incremental lift per paid dollar, so we always know whether the catalyst is working or we're just buying expensive views. If R3 ever shows paid isn't lifting organic, budgets get cut — the strategy enforces this automatically.

Honest caveats

  • Ranges, not promises. Niche matters enormously — finance pays 2–3× entertainment for both ad rates and sponsors.
  • Organic breakout is the gating factor: no budget fixes a channel with no winners. That's exactly why funding is winners-only (P2.1).
  • Timelines depend on hit rate. With ~90 videos/month, even a 5% breakout rate compounds fast — or doesn't, and the weekly review says so early enough to pivot before real money is spent.
  • Sponsor-rate and revenue-mix figures are industry estimates and creator reports, not official payout data — see the caveats in ../reference/monetization-research.md. Recalibrate every figure against actuals once revenue starts flowing (tech spec §7).
