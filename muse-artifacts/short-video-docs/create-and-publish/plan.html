Short-Video Content Pipeline — Plan

Owner: Balram Bandhu · Built & operated by: Muse Bandhu Date: 2026-09-23 · Updated: 2026-09-24 — T0.1 done; locked decisions incorporated Status: In build (Phase 0) Technical spec: techspec.md Tasks: tasks-balram.md (decisions & approvals — yours) · tasks-muse.md (build work — Muse's) Sibling systems: ../promotion/plan.md (paid + organic promotion — separate pipeline, separate docs) · ../reference/ (locked decisions, brand kit, guardrails, platform policies, research) · ../ledgers/ (operational records)

This document covers the create-and-publish pipeline only: ideation through publication. It is one of five independent pipelines, each with its own codebase, its own docs, and its own observability — create-and-publish (this), promotion, housekeeping, performance-monitoring, and earnings-improvement. They share a learning knowledge base (see §12). Locked decisions referenced here live in full in ../reference/decisions-log.md.

1. Vision

A fully automated short-video factory run by Muse: every day, multiple vertical short videos (30–60s) are ideated, scripted, voiced, rendered, assembled, and published to YouTube (Shorts), Instagram (Reels), Facebook (Reels), and TikTok — with zero day-to-day effort from you after setup. The system is a replicable template: launching channel #2, #3, … with a different theme is copy-and-configure, not a rebuild.

Default throughput (adjustable any time): 3 videos/day × 4 platforms (2 volume + 1 higher-effort hero; hero = 1 per 3 videos, rounding up — so a 1-video day is all hero).

2. Objectives

  • Publish 3 videos/day, 7 days/week, on all four platforms.
  • Grow subscribers/followers and views on each platform (numeric targets set after pilot data).
  • Keep production cost per video as low as possible — tracked, not guessed.
  • Promotion (separate pipeline) funds only proven organic winners: min cost, max impact.
  • Every video complies with the universal content guardrails and the original-media rule (see §3–§4); the topic engine enforces them at ideation, before any generation.
  • The pipeline watches itself: own failure tracking, failure-pattern learning, bounded self-healing, and severity-tiered alerts (see §12).

A weekly automated review decides which formats to scale and which to kill.

4. How videos get made

Each video goes through the same 7 stages. Stages 1–6 run unattended on schedule; stage 7 publishes.

  1. Ideation (topic engine). The engine autonomously finds candidate topics from: official product and feature announcements, new tools, pricing and cost changes, announced launches and event calendars, search/platform trends, and performance data from prior videos. Every candidate is compared against the full content ledger — near-duplicates are rejected, and meaningful episode-to-episode variation is preserved. Guardrails are enforced here, before scripting: candidates that violate the universal content guardrails (or the news-reactive scope for AI Technology Education) are rejected or flagged for your review. The engine reacts to unannounced news rather than pretending to predict it. During the pilot, candidates route through your approval gate. Output: a ranked queue of video ideas.
  2. Script. Muse writes a voiceover-ready script (~120–150 words ≈ 45–60s): hook in the first seconds, one idea per sentence for clean captions.
  3. Voiceover. Per-format policy set in calibration (tech task T1.15): full VO, VO-free (text + visuals carry it), or hybrid. Where VO is used, it's the channel's fixed narrator voice — the same voice every video = brand consistency.
  4. Visuals. For channel #1: AI-generated original illustrations (~10s clips each, 4–6 per video) with visual continuity, plus our own automated real screen captures of the tools/actions being explained, plus a 9:16 cover image. All generated media is original — nothing is pulled from the internet (anything else requires disclosure and your approval first).
  5. Assembly. Clips joined, captions burned in, voiceover mixed over background audio. Final spec: 1080×1920, 30–60s.
  6. Quality check (automated). Duration, resolution, file health, frame spot-checks, caption sync. Anything broken is quarantined — never published.
  7. Packaging. Title, description, hashtags, and AI-content disclosure tuned per platform, not copied: YouTube gets search-keyword titles, TikTok gets trend-native captions, Instagram gets share/save hooks. Sponsor/affiliate hooks: videos that carry sponsor or affiliate integrations include the required description links/text, pinned comment, and on-screen/audio integration per the sponsor obligations ledger (../ledgers/sponsor-obligations.md); affiliate links come from the tracked link hub with CTA rotation.

Formats

Proven structures the pipeline supports: hook → 3 beats → CTA (listicles), relatable story/skit, myth-vs-fact hot takes, weekly trend-jacks in the niche, seamless loops (the end flows into the start — average watch time over 100%), evergreen explainers ("what is X" — compounds via search for months), and remix/response — an original take responding to a larger creator's video in-niche (borrows their audience; Muse shortlists targets weekly, you approve). Every video follows the same skeleton: 0–3s hook, 3–40s payoff, last 3s call-to-action. Hero tier: 1 of every 3 videos is a hero — more clips, tighter QC, hook variants tested; the rest are volume. (At reduced cadence the ratio holds: 1 hero per 3 videos, rounding up.)

5. Publishing

  • One schedule, four platforms. Publish slots (default 08:00, 13:00, 19:00 ET — adjustable) fire automatically; each video goes to YouTube, Instagram, Facebook, and TikTok.
  • Warm-up ramp. New accounts start slow — posting 3 videos/day from day one on a fresh account risks spam flags and throttled distribution. The pilot doubles as warm-up: week 1 at 1 video/day, week 2 at 2/day, full cadence from week 3. Cross-platform post times are staggered, not simultaneous.
  • Never a duplicate. Each video publishes to each platform at most once; reruns resume where they stopped.
  • 3-day buffer. The generator keeps three days of ready videos, so a bad generation day never means a missed publish day.
  • Broken stays unpublished. Failed publishes don't auto-retry blindly; they land in a daily digest for your review.
  • Dayparted by data. Default slots 08:00/13:00/19:00 ET; the weekly review retunes times per platform from audience-online analytics — free reach, zero cost.
  • Native scheduling where supported (YouTube/Facebook auto-publish at the slot time).
  • Sponsor-gated alterations. Hiding, unlisting, or materially altering a published video is blocked while its sponsor obligations are active unless the contract explicitly allows it — the sponsor obligations ledger is checked before any such action.

Playlists (YouTube-first)

  • Category playlists. One playlist per content category — categories come from the format taxonomy locked in T0.2 (e.g. explainers, myth-vs-fact, stories). Every published video is auto-added to its category playlist at publish time 🟢. The channel page stays organized, viewers get bingeable shelves, and playlists add a second discovery surface (search/SEO) — all with zero ongoing effort.
  • Long-form shelf. Weekly long-form episodes auto-collect into a dedicated "Deep dives" playlist 🟢.
  • "Start here" playlist. Built rule-based from R1 performance scores: the current top-performing videos, refreshed monthly 🟢 — a binge path that converts new subscribers.
  • Series playlists. Multi-part videos grouped in watch order 🟢 (order comes from series metadata set at creation).
  • TikTok playlists where the feature is available on the account 🟠 (partially manual — API support is limited, fallback is manual add). Instagram and Facebook have no video-playlist equivalent — skipped there.
  • New playlist categories need your ok 🟡 — categories mirror the channel's format taxonomy, so adding one is a channel-structure decision, not a background tweak.

6. Your control panel — single-prompt controls

  • Cadence: "2 videos a day this week", "back to 3 a day for October", "just one video today" — for a day, a week, a month, or until you change it. Scoped changes revert automatically.
  • Review mode: "send this one for review first", "turn review back on for this week".
  • Emergency stop: "pause all publishing now" — everything halts until you say resume.
  • Video actions by reference (see §7): "unpublish 2026.09.24.3", "report 2026.09.24.3", "promote 2026.09.24.3".
  • Campaign controls live in the promotion plan.

7. Video references

Every video gets a short reference at creation: <channel>.yyyy.MM.dd.N (e.g. ainews.2026.09.24.3; channel prefix optional with a single channel; N = 1–5 per day). The ref is printed as the last line of every description/caption — searchable by you in YouTube Studio / Business Suite, effectively invisible to viewers — and is the key for everything: status checks, unpublish, remove, adjust-and-republish, comment controls, per-video reports, promotion handoff, review. Destructive actions always ask first. (Storage and resolution mechanics: tech spec §7.)

8. Review flow (initial weeks only)

Until the generated content fully matches your vision, every video goes through admin review: uploaded as unlisted to YouTube (chat preview for IG/FB/TikTok), you get the link, you say "approve" — it goes public. Each review is also how you steer the output: your corrections feed back into the channel's style guide. When the output consistently matches the vision, you say "lock the vision" → review switches off and publishing goes fully automatic. Re-enable any time with a prompt.

Reviews are batched: each day's videos arrive in one session, not one ping per video — expect ~15–20 min/day of your time during the pilot, less after vision-lock.

9. Analytics, reports & feedback loop

This pipeline defines its own operational reports (built in tech task T1.21): a daily production report (videos made, QC pass/reject with rejection reasons, buffer health, cost per video, generation usage vs allowance) and a weekly content performance summary (per-video velocity, retention, engagement, format leaderboard, breakout flags). These feed the shared R1 cross-pipeline rollup owned by the performance-monitoring pipeline's reporting framework — one framework, many detectors; see ../reference/architecture.md.

  • Nightly: per-video views, retention, likes, comments, shares, and follows/subs collected automatically.
  • Weekly (Sundays): a digest in chat — top/bottom 3 videos per platform with why (hook retention, view duration), a format leaderboard, and concrete calls: scale (make variants), kill (retire), test (try one new format).
  • Winners feed back in: successful hooks and formats get priority in the idea queue — the system learns weekly. Every video also writes its performance outcome back to the shared learning knowledge base; learning-driven pipeline changes need your approval during the pilot (bounded automatic adjustments only after full automation is authorized).
  • Project circuit breaker: at week 4 and week 8, the digest carries an explicit go / pivot / pause verdict for the channel against preset gates (defaults: median views per video and follower growth vs. the niche baselines from T0.1 — tuned once the niche is locked). A dead niche gets pivoted before it burns months, not after. Backup niches (history/mythology, crime news) are the pivot options.
  • Hook-variant testing: new formats ship with 2–3 different first-3s hooks; retention data picks the winner, which becomes the template.
  • Long-form companion (weekly): the week's top Shorts recut as an 8–12 min YouTube deep-dive/compilation. ⚠️ TO-VERIFY: the working claim that long-form RPM ≈ 50× Shorts is an open audit item — do not treat as fact until verified against current official/reliable sources.
  • Winner recycling (monthly): the top 10% of videos get a second life — cutdowns (best 15s as a new Short) and reposts of proven videos with fresh hooks/covers after 30 days. ("Best of" compilations are owned by the weekly long-form job.) Winners keep working; the ledger tracks each recycle against its original ref.
  • Stale-content policy (locked 2026-09-24): nothing is ever deleted. A monthly housekeeping review checks every video for accuracy, relevance, links, sponsor obligations, and newer versions. Slightly stale content gets an updated caption/description + links, a pinned comment pointing to the newer version, and cards/end screens where supported. A stale long-form segment is trimmed where supported and redirected. Thoroughly outdated content gets a full remake as a new upload — and the old version is hidden/unlisted only if leaving it public would mislead or be inaccurate. Sponsor obligations always gate any hide/alter action. Full triage ladder: §12 and ../reference/decisions-log.md.
  • On demand: "how did this week's videos do?" any time.

11. Multi-channel expansion

Channel #2+ is a checklist, not a project: copy the template folder, fill in the new theme's brand config (Muse drafts from a 5-minute brief you approve), create the four accounts, connect credentials, stagger publish times, run a 2-week pilot, then full-auto. Shared across channels: publisher integrations, scripts, audio library. Per-channel: theme, voice, accounts, config.

Locked operating method

  • One owner per task: you = decisions, manual work, approvals; Muse = technical work.
  • One task at a time, in dependency order. Each result is presented; you reply "adjust" or "done". No checkbox is ticked and no dependent work starts until you say "done".
  • Upfront and recurring costs are stated on every task.
  • 🟢/🟡/🔴/🟠 automation indicators are preserved on every task.
  • Pilot publishing stays behind per-video approval; full automation begins only after "go auto" / "lock the vision" (or equivalent). "Turn review back on" restores review. "Pause all publishing now" halts everything until you say resume.
  • Cadence is adjustable by one prompt for a day, week, month, or indefinitely.

Universal content guardrails

Apply to all current and future niches and channels: no sensitive or objectionable content; nothing involving children; no current politics; nothing potentially controversial or otherwise inappropriate. The topic engine enforces these at ideation; violations are rejected or flagged for your review. Full text: ../reference/content-guardrails.md.

Original-media rule

All generated video and audio is original. No existing video or audio from the internet is used without explicit disclosure and your approval. Real-human UGC is ruled out (at least for now) — no real person on camera. If UGC is ever pursued, it is the AI-avatar/AI-generated tier only, with appropriate disclosure — or UGC is deprioritized in favour of affiliates and later sponsorships.

YouTube originality risk

Channels posting high volumes of AI-made content face extra scrutiny at monetization review (repetitious-content policy). Mitigation: format-variety quota (no single format >40% of weekly output), logged human creative input per video (your calibration decisions + review corrections count), AI-content disclosure on every upload. Current platform stance verified at build (tech spec §1.5). ⚠️ TO-VERIFY: claims that YPP thresholds will change in February 2027, and that finance is categorically blocked for AI personas, are open audit items — not settled facts.

This pipeline's own observability

This pipeline watches itself, independently of the other four:

  • Own tracking: every bug, failure, and performance event is logged against this pipeline (generation failures, QC rejects by category, publish failures, quota events).
  • Failure-pattern history: recurring patterns are captured and used — QC-rejection categories feed back into generation prompts and the shared learning knowledge base.
  • Self-healing (bounded): predefined mechanisms with automated execution plans — generation retries ≤3 with backoff, fallback models, quarantine of broken outputs. Repeated failures escalate instead of looping forever.
  • Anomaly detection: watches for unfavorable conditions, exception scenarios, weak growth, and bottlenecks (e.g. buffer draining, quota burn running hot, a format's retention collapsing).
  • Severity-tiered alerts: P1 (needs you now — e.g. publishing fully halted), P2 (needs a decision soon — e.g. repeated healing failures), P3 (informational — e.g. a format trending down). Tiers are based on importance, occurrence rate, urgency, and the need for human review/approval/intervention. Thresholds are locked with you in T0.5.
