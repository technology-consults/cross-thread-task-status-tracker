Housekeeping Pipeline — Plan

Owner: Balram Bandhu · Built & operated by: Muse Bandhu Date: 2026-09-24 · Status: Draft for review Technical spec: techspec.md Tasks: tasks-balram.md (decisions & approvals — yours) · tasks-muse.md (build work — Muse's)

1. Vision

Published content never rots silently. Every video across every platform is re-examined on a monthly cadence: accuracy, relevance, links, sponsor obligations, and whether a newer version exists. Stale content is updated, redirected, trimmed, or remade — never deleted — and nothing is hidden or altered while a sponsor obligation is still live. The channel stays trustworthy as the technology it covers moves on.

2. Objectives

  • Review 100% of published videos monthly, across YouTube, Instagram, Facebook, and TikTok.
  • Catch and fix stale content before viewers do: broken links, outdated pricing/features, superseded topics.
  • Honor every sponsor obligation to the letter — no hide, unlist, or material alteration before the minimum-live date without contract approval.
  • Keep a clean, queryable record of every review decision (what was checked, what was done, why).
  • Feed failure patterns back into the shared learning knowledge base (which topics age fastest, which link sources rot).

3. Scope

All published videos on all four platforms, for every channel. This pipeline does not create content and does not spend money — it maintains what's already live. Remakes it orders become new uploads and are produced through the create-and-publish pipeline; housekeeping tracks them, it doesn't render them.

4. The monthly workflow — the triage ladder

4.1 The review pass

Once a month, on a fixed schedule, the pipeline walks the full content ledger and checks each video for:

  1. Accuracy — are the facts, prices, features, and claims still true?
  2. Relevance — is the topic still worth a viewer's time?
  3. Links — do description links resolve? Any link rot or redirects to dead pages?
  4. Sponsor obligations — are required links, pinned comments, on-screen integrations still in place and compliant?
  5. Newer versions — does a newer video on this channel cover the same topic better?

4.2 The ladder

Every video lands on exactly one rung:

  • (a) Still broadly accurate → leave public. Technology evolved, the video is still fine. No action. Logged as reviewed.
  • (b) Slightly stale → update in place. Refresh the caption/description and links; add a pinned comment pointing to the newer version; on YouTube long-form, add cards/end screens pointing onward where supported (TO-VERIFY current support). The video stays public with a clear path to the fresher take.
  • (c) One stale segment → trim and redirect. Where the platform supports trimming (TO-VERIFY per platform), cut the stale segment and redirect viewers to updated content via caption/pinned comment. Note: published footage generally cannot be replaced or spliced — trim only removes.
  • (d) Thoroughly outdated → full remake as a NEW upload. Housekeeping flags it; the create-and-publish pipeline produces the remake. The old version is hidden/unlisted only if leaving it public would mislead or be inaccurate. Otherwise it stays public with redirect pointers (rung b treatment).

4.3 The never-delete rule

Nothing is ever deleted. Not underperformers, not embarrassments, not outdated takes. Deletion destroys watch-time history, breaks embeds, and looks like a cover-up. Hide/unlist is the strongest action this pipeline takes, and only on rung (d) with your approval.

4.4 Comments stay enabled

Feedback is signal. Hiding comments to dodge criticism is not a housekeeping action — objections and corrections in comments feed the learning knowledge base and can trigger a re-review.

5. Sponsor obligations — the gate

The sponsor/deal obligations ledger (ledgers/sponsor-obligations.md, schema in techspec §5) records per video: sponsor/brand, contract and campaign ID, minimum-live-until date, required description links/text, pinned-comment requirements, on-screen/audio integration, usage rights, exclusivity, approval/contact record.

The gate: any action that would hide, unlist, or materially alter a video is blocked while obligations remain active, unless explicitly approved under the contract. The pipeline checks the ledger before every rung-(b/c/d) action; a live obligation routes the decision to you, not around you.

7. Link hygiene

Links rot faster than facts. The link checker runs more often than the full review (weekly): every tracked link in every description is resolved; failures are retried on a bounded budget, then flagged. A spike in failures from one domain is an anomaly (techspec §6), not just a list of broken links — it may mean a whole affiliate program or tool shut down.

8. Learning hooks

This pipeline reads from and writes to the shared learning knowledge base (winning hooks/formats/topics, failure patterns — maintained by the performance-monitoring pipeline). Housekeeping writes: which topics aged fastest, which link domains rotted, which rung each video landed on and why, remake performance vs. original. During the pilot, learning-driven changes need your approval.

9. Your control panel — single-prompt controls

  • Run now / schedule: "run housekeeping now", "housekeeping first of the month" — the monthly run is automatic; on-demand runs anytime.
  • Scope: "review just September's videos", "housekeeping for channel 1 only".
  • Pause: "pause housekeeping" halts scheduled runs until you resume. (Note: "pause all publishing now" halts publishing; housekeeping is separate — say "pause everything" to stop both.)

10. Approvals & guardrails

Action Mode
Monthly review run Automatic 🟢
Rung (a) leave public / rung (b) in-place updates Automatic 🟢
Rung (c) trim + redirect Your approval per video 🟡
Rung (d) remake as new upload Your approval per video 🟡
Hide/unlist/archive any video Your approval per video 🟡
Sponsor-conflicted action (obligation live) Your call, always 🟡
Learning-driven process changes (pilot) Your approval 🟡

Muse never: deletes a video, hides/unlists without your approval, alters a video under active sponsor obligation without contract approval, or invents review results.

11. Observability & self-healing (summary)

Like every pipeline, housekeeping has its own bug, failure, and performance tracking with captured failure-pattern history; predefined self-healing mechanisms with automated execution plans (bounded retry budgets, fallbacks, quarantine); escalation when repeated healing fails; anomaly detection (broken-link spikes, review backlog growth); and severity-tiered notifications. Details in techspec §6.

12. Reports

Housekeeping's own operational reports (defined here, produced by this pipeline): the monthly review outcome — videos reviewed, and counts per rung (left public / updated / trimmed / remade / unlisted), link-rot summary, sponsor-obligation compliance check. Delivered in chat after each run. Cross-pipeline rollups (R2/R3) are owned by performance-monitoring.

13. What needs your hands vs what runs itself

  • You, per action: approve trims, remakes, and any hide/unlist; resolve sponsor conflicts; approve learning-driven changes in the pilot. Expect a short batch session after each monthly run.
  • Muse, everything else: inventory, checks, link hygiene, ledger enforcement, in-place updates, reports, self-healing.
  • Honest limits: platform features (pin, trim, cards, archive) are TO-VERIFY — anything unproven becomes an explicit manual step, never a silent skip.

14. Costs

Upfront: $0 out-of-pocket (your time only). Recurring: draws on your Muse plan for the monthly run + weekly link checks (light compute — page fetches and API calls, well within quotas). API calls $0 within quotas. No ad spend, no subscriptions, no per-video fees in this pipeline.

15. Timeline

  • Build (with the other pipelines): inventory + check engine first, then triage actions, then ledger gate, then observability.
  • First run: one full review cycle after create-and-publish has published videos worth reviewing (no earlier than week 5 of the main pipeline).
  • Steady state: monthly review + weekly link checks, fully automatic except your per-action approvals.

16. Open decisions (yours)

  2. Rung (b) in-place updates: fully automatic, or batched for your glance first?
  3. Remake threshold: how outdated is "thoroughly outdated" — your call per video, or do you want written criteria?
