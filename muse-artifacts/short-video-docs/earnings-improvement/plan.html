Earnings Improvement Pipeline — Plan

Owner: Balram Bandhu · Built & operated by: Muse Bandhu Date: 2026-09-24 · Status: Draft for review Technical spec: techspec.md Tasks: tasks-balram.md (decisions & approvals — yours) · tasks-muse.md (build work — Muse's)

1. Vision

Every video earns as much as it honestly can, and every dollar is traceable to the video, topic, format, and platform that produced it. Sponsorships arrive through a real pipeline — prospected, pitched, negotiated, and closed — not wished for. Affiliates are tracked click-to-conversion. Costs are watched as closely as revenue. The objective throughout: min cost, max impact.

2. Objectives

  • Attribute revenue by video, topic, format, and platform — know what earns, not just what trends.
  • Run the affiliate layer: program research, tracked links, CTA rotation, click/conversion tracking.
  • Run the sponsor pipeline end-to-end: prospecting → outreach → negotiation → close, with a rate-card framework and an auto-generated media kit built from real metrics.
  • Track cost-per-video and flag pricing changes in the tools the pipeline depends on.
  • Monitor the seasonal ad market so monetization moves with it, not after it.
  • Keep platform-dependence risk visible — no single platform should be able to silently become the whole business.

3. Scope

Revenue-side work only. This pipeline does not create videos, does not buy ads, and does not review content. Boundaries:

  • Promotion spends money to amplify winners; earnings measures what comes back. Promotion reports spend; earnings reports revenue; performance-monitoring shows both side by side.
  • Housekeeping enforces the sponsor obligations ledger; earnings writes new entries into it when deals close.

4. Revenue attribution

Every revenue event — platform ad payout, affiliate commission, sponsor payment — is attributed to video ref, topic, format, and platform where the data allows. Attribution confidence is recorded (direct / inferred / unknown); inferred attribution is labeled, never presented as exact. Outputs: which topics and formats earn per view, which platforms pay, where the next dollar of effort goes.

5. Affiliate program

  • Research & applications: Muse researches affiliate programs fitting the AI-tech-education niche (and later niches), shortlists by fit and terms — you approve and submit applications (manual gate, §10).
  • Link hub: one hub page with tracked links per program/product; every video description links through the hub, never raw.
  • Tracked links: click and conversion tracking per link, per video that carried it.
  • CTA rotation: calls-to-action rotate by performance data — winning CTAs get more slots; the rotation rules read the learning KB.
  • All affiliate content follows the original-media rule and carries proper disclosure.

6. Sponsor pipeline

Stages: prospecting → outreach → negotiation → close.

  • Prospecting: sponsor prospect shortlist generated from niche fit, audience match, and past sponsor behavior in the space.
  • Outreach: personalized templates + send tracking. Sending is a manual gate (§10) — Muse drafts, you send (or explicitly authorize).
  • Negotiation & close: manual gates. Muse prepares the numbers and the terms; you talk and sign.

Sponsorship routes (all five stay open):

  1. Inbound discovery — media kit + contact path public; inbound inquiries triaged automatically.
  2. Personalized outbound pitching — researched, per-brand pitches.
  3. Creator marketplaces — profiles maintained, briefs answered.
  4. Agencies / MCNs — engaged only with your approval; commission terms reviewed per deal.
  5. UGC / content-production deals — AI-avatar/AI-generated tier only, with disclosure. Real-human UGC is ruled out for now — no real person on camera.

7. Media kit & rate card

  • Media kit: auto-generated from real channel metrics (pulled from performance-monitoring — never hand-written numbers), refreshed monthly. Publishing it externally needs your approval.
  • Rate-card framework: formula-driven starting points from audience size, engagement, and niche benchmarks. Industry rate figures are estimates, not guarantees — the card presents ranges with the basis shown, not promises.

8. Link hub & CTA rotation

Covered in §5; technically: the hub is a versioned page, links carry per-video tracking parameters, rotation weights update from conversion data on a weekly cadence. Broken hub links are housekeeping's link-checker's problem — the two pipelines share the link inventory.

9. Seasonal ad-market monitoring & cost tracking

  • Seasonal ad market: tracks advertiser-demand seasonality (e.g. Q4 CPM strength) so publishing and sponsorship pushes lean into high- demand windows. Notes feed R3 via performance-monitoring.
  • Cost-per-video tracking: every video's generation cost logged; pricing-change alerts when a dependency (model, API, tool) changes price — cost drift is caught in days, not at month-end.

10. Manual gates — yours, always

Gate Why it's manual
Identity / tax details for affiliate or sponsor onboarding Your personal information — never handled by automation 🟡
Affiliate program applications Submitted in your name 🟡
Outreach sending Sent in your voice, your relationships 🟡
Negotiation Judgment + relationships 🟡
Closing deals Signature authority 🟡
Publishing the media kit externally Your numbers, your approval 🟡

Muse drafts everything up to the gate: research, shortlists, templates, numbers, terms. Nothing crosses a gate without you.

11. Learning hooks

This pipeline reads from and writes to the shared learning knowledge base: affiliate click/conversion data, CTA performance, sponsor response rates by outreach variant, revenue-by-topic/format findings, cost observations. During the pilot, learning-driven changes (e.g. CTA rotation rule changes) need your approval.

12. Observability & self-healing (summary)

Own bug/failure/performance tracking with failure-pattern history; predefined self-healing (bounded retry budgets on dashboard pulls and link checks, fallback to manual CSV import where an API is unavailable, quarantine of corrupt attribution batches); escalation on repeated failure; anomaly detection (revenue drops, click-through collapse, a sponsor going quiet mid-campaign); severity-tiered alerts via the shared framework. Details in techspec §13.

13. Reports

This pipeline's own operational reports: revenue attribution (by video/topic/format/platform), affiliate performance (clicks, conversions, per-link, per-video), sponsor pipeline status (stage per prospect/deal), cost-per-video trends, pricing-change alerts. Delivered in chat on a weekly cadence (folded into R2) with monthly depth (R3). Cross-pipeline rollups remain performance-monitoring's job.

14. What needs your hands vs what runs itself

  • You, decisions: the six manual gates (§10); rate-card ranges; which affiliate programs to join; which sponsors to pursue; pricing and terms calls.
  • Muse, everything else: research, tracking, attribution, rotation, monitoring, reports, self-healing.
  • Honest limits: affiliate dashboard APIs vary wildly — where no API exists, ingestion is a documented manual CSV step, not a silent gap. Sponsor rates and timelines are industry estimates until your data replaces them. No revenue figure is ever projected as a promise.

15. Costs

Upfront: $0 out-of-pocket (your time only). Recurring: draws on your Muse plan (tracking, attribution, report generation — light compute; API calls $0 within quotas). Affiliate programs cost $0 to join. Creator marketplaces are free to join. If you engage an agency/MCN, expect commission (typically 10–20% of deal value — industry estimate, verify per agreement); that spend needs your per-deal approval and is reported against the revenue it produced.

16. Timeline

  • Build: attribution model + tracked links first, then affiliate layer, then sponsor pipeline tooling, then media kit/rate card, then monitoring.
  • Affiliate applications: once pilot videos exist and the media kit has real numbers (no earlier than week 5 of create-and-publish).
  • Sponsor outreach: when audience metrics support a pitch — your call, informed by R3, not by a calendar date.

17. Open decisions (yours)

  1. Affiliate programs: which categories are in-bounds for AI tech education (tools, courses, hardware)?
  2. Sponsor categories to exclude, if any.
  3. Rate-card basis: views, subscribers, or engagement-weighted?
  4. Agency/MCN route: open to introductions, or direct-only for now?
